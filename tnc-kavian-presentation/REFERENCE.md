# Kavian presentation for The New Centre — working reference

**Created and updated:** 22 September 2026  
**Purpose:** A single, traceable reference for ideating, developing, and finalizing the Kavian presentation requested after Kaveh Momeni's first scholarship interview. This file records what the application actually said, what Kaveh has since described, what the current concept proposes, and which presentation decisions remain open. It is an internal working reference, not a slide script or proof that the civic service has launched.

## 1. The situation and the audience

Kaveh applied for a scholarship to The New Centre for Research & Practice's **Information Architecture & Intelligence Design** certificate program for Fall/Winter 2026–27. The submitted application materials frame Kavian as a **design inquiry**: an evidence-first, anti-capture public accountability platform concerned with who controls the architecture of public knowledge. They do not present Kavian as a deployed product. The application's other major question is creativity: whether intelligence can transform a possibility space instead of only searching within one.

**New information from Kaveh, 22 September 2026:** In the first interview, the interviewers reacted strongly to Kavian and asked him to create a presentation about the project. There is no interview transcript, written brief, or specified duration, format, deadline, evaluation rubric, or attendee list in this folder. Their reaction and request are Kaveh's firsthand report. The presentation should therefore be built for an intellectually serious TNC audience while leaving its precise format open until clarified.

The relevant audience is likely interested in intelligence, information, design, agency, politics, culture, and the practice of building systems that can be critically examined. This is an inference from the program research and application materials, not a documented description of who will attend the presentation. The strongest material for this audience is the connection between a philosophical question and a buildable civic system: **who decides what counts as knowledge, and where can that decision be challenged?**

## 2. The application narrative that introduced Kavian

The [final letter of intent](../tnc-scholarship/final/Kaveh_Momeni_TNC_Letter_of_Intent.md) describes Kaveh's path from being excluded from computer-engineering study in Iran, through infrastructure and organizational systems, to production AI. It argues that systems can move judgment upstream: a feature set, label, or definition of a problem can quietly determine what later appears to be an automatic decision. In the letter, information architecture means **where the decision about what counts is made, and by whom**.

The letter brings together two research questions. One concerns creativity and whether generative systems transform a space of possibilities. The other concerns civic knowledge and the ability to know what is happening under conditions of information control. Kavian is introduced as the response to the second question. The letter calls it an evidence-first, anti-capture design inquiry in which claims are typed, sourced, and answerable, and says its purpose is to return to people the power to know what is happening to them.

The [writing sample, *Redrawing the Space*](../tnc-scholarship/final/Kaveh_Momeni_TNC_Writing_Sample.md), develops the bridge more fully:

- **Personal and artistic origin:** the experience of exclusion, and the santur as an example of making something new within material and cultural constraints.
- **Professional discovery:** production systems can centralize human judgment inside labels and objectives, then make that judgment hard to see. A human review gate can say that the system answered the data correctly but asked the wrong question.
- **Conceptual distinction:** novelty, statistical surprise, and invention are different. The essay explores whether invention changes the rules by which outcomes are judged.
- **Cybernetic lens:** Wiener on information and feedback; Ashby on requisite variety; Beer on assessing a system by what it does; Bateson on consequential difference. These are the essay's interpretive tools, not settled proofs of the design.
- **Civic turn:** the essay describes information restriction during violence in Iran as an exercise of power over what can be known and remembered. It presents Kavian as a set of principles, an architecture, and a personal commitment, expressly saying it is not yet a product.
- **Shared question:** in art, organizations, and public life, can people revise the model of reality and the rules of evaluation, or are they confined to choices already offered by a system?

The writing sample names early Kavian safeguards: types for claims (fact, estimate, analysis, proposal), sources and confidence levels, a right of reply, auditable process, a human authority to refuse publication, and a founder sunset clause. It does not describe the later, more developed human network for understanding and evaluation. The presentation can show this as a genuine evolution of the inquiry.

## 3. Kavian as currently formulated

The current working definition is in the [English concept](../concept/kavian-concept-en.md) and [Persian concept](../concept/kavian-concept-fa.md). These documents were developed after the application packet and expanded through Kaveh's conversation on 22 September 2026. They describe Kavian as a public service connecting people's lived experience, human judgment, evidence, institutional memory, and AI. It helps people understand public problems, examine power, compare possible responses, see the likely costs and consequences of choices, and track what happens after a decision. Its purpose is to improve people's capacity to make decisions for themselves, not to have a system or an adviser make those decisions for them. It begins with the needs and freedoms of people in Iran and remains open to learning from, and serving, other communities.

The core unit is a **living public dossier** about a claim, report, promise, decision, problem, program, or proposal. A dossier connects evidence, relevant institutions and interests, affected people, interpretations, possible actions, replies, corrections, and outcomes over time. It can make a provisional finding while showing what remains uncertain and what could change that finding. Its value is both immediate understanding and durable civic memory.

Current conceptual capacities:

| Capacity | Contribution | Essential boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Public participation and lived experience | Raise problems, supply context and evidence, contest conclusions, and follow outcomes | Affected people need a direct route in; they do not require an expert title |
| Evidence and memory | Connect claims, sources, decisions, institutions, and consequences over time | Preserve provenance, corrections, and uncertainty |
| AI assistance | Search, extract, compare, link, question, draft, and translate | AI output does not become authority simply because it is systematic |
| Network of advisers | Deepen questions and review evidence, interpretation, options, and consequences | Relevant competence matters, but advisers do not replace public choice |
| Publication and paths to action | Explain findings, develop options, propose and advocate with accountable human ownership | Keep evidence, interpretation, and recommendation distinguishable |
| Kavian's own governance | Set rules, review conflicts, handle appeals, and limit founder, donor, and operator power | The people running the service must themselves be answerable |

### The network of advisers

Kaveh clarified during the concept work that this is a **cross-cutting module**, not just a governing council or a final approval board. In conversation he preferred the working name **network of advisers** (*shabakeh-ye moshaveran*) over “elites,” which risks a hierarchy over the public, and “human evaluators,” which makes the role too narrow. The name should be simple while communicating that these people know their work. It could involve researchers, writers, public intellectuals, economists, sociologists, literary figures, technical specialists, civic practitioners, and others with relevant knowledge or experience, including deliberately contrasting perspectives. It can help at any point: framing a problem, challenging a source, checking an analysis, identifying missing human consequences, assessing a proposed solution, or deciding what deserves further investigation.

Kaveh described a more concrete circulation mechanism. Incoming news, documents, datasets, and public accounts contribute to **reviewable artifacts** within a living dossier. An artifact is a bounded object for inquiry, not a verdict. Possible forms for design exploration include a claim-and-evidence card, an issue timeline, a comparison of conflicting accounts, an open-question list, or a description of options and possible effects. These examples are proposed formats, not recorded implementation. An artifact may be sent to one or several advisers across relevant, sometimes conflicting fields and perspectives. Advisers provide criticism, interpretation, missing context, new evidence, or a challenge to the framing. Their feedback is matched back to the artifact and synthesized into a public-facing account of support, uncertainty, agreement, and disagreement. The goal is a more comprehensive view for the person trying to understand the issue, not a claim that every viewpoint has equal evidential standing or that any synthesis is final.

The artifact route is potentially iterative: an adviser can return an artifact for revision; several advisers can expose a conflict about evidence, interpretation, or values; directly affected people can contest the synthesis; later evidence can change the dossier. Routing, versioning, attribution, privacy, compensation, response rights, and the authority to publish are still design questions. The system must make plural views legible without flattening them into a single false consensus.

A possible method draws on Delphi: independent assessments, structured feedback, reconsideration, and a record of both agreement and disagreement. **Policy Delphi** is particularly relevant for contested civic questions because it can surface the reasons for divergence rather than aim only at consensus. Other candidate methods are structured expert elicitation, multi-criteria decision analysis, and sensitivity analysis. They answer different questions: how to elicit judgments, how to compare options on explicit criteria, and how strongly a result depends on weights or assumptions. A possible weighting scheme for some expert estimates would depend on the question, relevant expertise, applicable experience, and disclosed conflicts. **The selection process, weights, criteria, decision rules, appeal route, and treatment of disagreement are still unbuilt design questions.** A factual claim's credibility depends on evidence and method; an estimate under uncertainty may benefit from structured judgment; a public value choice belongs to affected people and the wider public. No adviser weight can confer democratic legitimacy by itself.

This methodological list is a research agenda, not a declaration that any method has been implemented. [RAND's Delphi guidance](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/tools/TLA3000/TLA3082-1/RAND_TLA3082-1.pdf) describes iteration and controlled feedback; [research on Policy Delphi](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/when-experts-disagree-using-the-policy-delphi-method-to-analyse-d/) shows its use in presenting disagreement. A method should be chosen only after specifying the question and what kind of judgment it is meant to support.

### The reported technical architecture and its limits

In the latest conversation, Kaveh supplied a technical story not contained in the application packet: a **multi-agent architecture** using LangChain and LangGraph, with LangSmith and Langfuse named for tracing, observability, and evaluation. He reported that the system runs on Vercel, GitHub supports the code, and substantial observability work has already been done. These are his current descriptions of the technical layer. The local concept and scholarship files do not include the relevant repository, deployment, traces, access to those services, or an implementation audit. The presentation must not turn his account into an independently verified claim that all these components are configured, secure, or serving users.

The public-interest reason for observability is especially important: a dossier needs to preserve a path from an input and source version to an artifact, model action, adviser response, publication decision, correction, and observed consequence. A useful trace should answer who or what changed an assessment and why. But full public logs could expose vulnerable contributors, private evidence, or security-sensitive information. The design therefore needs layered visibility, consent and retention rules, redaction, access control, and a way to contest a summary or correction. These are design implications, not claims that a working privacy architecture exists.

Kaveh sees open-source components as potential support for independence and inspectability in an academic setting. This is a **potential presentation argument**, not an established preference of the actual TNC interviewers. The distinction matters: [LangChain](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain) and [LangGraph](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph) are open-source; [Langfuse's core](https://langfuse.com/handbook/chapters/open-source) is open-source and self-hostable, with some commercial features; the [LangSmith client SDK](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langsmith-sdk) is open-source, but the [LangSmith platform](https://smith.langchain.com/terms-of-service.pdf) should not be called open-source. Vercel and GitHub are external hosting and collaboration services. Open-source code, Kavian's own auditability, data control, and independence from providers are separate properties. An honest presentation can say the architecture is intended to remain inspectable and replaceable, then show what would actually secure those properties.

Kaveh introduced a class of small decision models for structured steps, beside language models. The ability is what matters: answer a fixed question and return a probability, without writing the public account. **Jev**, from TypeSafe, is a hosted example. [TypeSafe's introduction](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) describes typed answers with probabilities; its public release was announced on 15 September 2026, and the [service terms](https://typesafe.ai/legal/mca) describe a licensed hosted API. **Laya** ([convaiinnovations/laya](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya)) is an open-weight model of the same kind, under Apache 2.0. Neither is a general text-generating model, and neither output is a forecast of social consequences or a verdict. Claims of speed, cost, calibration, or reliability, from either maker, are not validated on Kavian's tasks, languages, data, or safety requirements. The defensible role is a **replaceable component for bounded structured judgments**, not “the brain of Kavian.” Open weights are not an audit of the system around them. The design question stays: which judgments can be typed, which need evidence, and which must remain with people.

Status distinctions to preserve in every presentation draft:

| Element | What is supported now | What is not established here |
|---|---|---|
| Public-interest purpose and living dossiers | Current Kavian concept and Kaveh's clarification | Live dossiers or measured public impact |
| Network of advisers and artifact circulation | Current design account from Kaveh | Recruitment, operating process, selection rules, or quality outcomes |
| Delphi, multi-criteria analysis, sensitivity analysis | Candidate methods for design and testing | Implemented procedure or validated weights |
| Multi-agent stack, Vercel, GitHub, observability | Kaveh's firsthand architectural report | Configuration, deployment state, tracing quality, or security audit |
| Decision-model slot: Jev hosted, Laya open weights | The ability, as described by each maker | Either model on Kavian’s Persian civic text, or provider independence |

The newer concept explicitly permits Kavian to analyze, propose, and advocate. The TNC presentation should explain how this can be done responsibly: show the basis of a proposal, disclose the human owner of an advocacy decision, assess possible harm, allow replies and correction, and avoid claiming to speak for all people. The earlier [Dreamprint exploration](../archive/2026-09-22/dreamprint/kavian-e0-p3.md) tested a narrower memory-only mandate, but Kaveh confirmed that it was an exploration, not the final boundary of Kavian.

### Scope and status

Kavian is **multi-domain by design**. Water was an early example for testing a smaller problem, described in the [archived water MVP](../archive/2026-09-22/kavian-mvp-water.md); it is not the definition of the service or a required focus for the TNC presentation. Domains may include health, education, economy, environment, culture, public services, rights, and the governance of technology. Connections across domains are part of the point.

The available project files are concept and strategy documents, presentation-style HTML explorations, and a water pilot specification. They do **not** themselves establish a running civic platform, recruited adviser network, deployed agent system, validated Delphi weights, live public dossiers, or demonstrated societal impact. Kaveh has subsequently reported a more specific technical architecture, but no implementation evidence was available to this reference. The presentation should distinguish **application text**, **founder-reported technical work**, **proposed architecture**, and **questions to investigate or prototype**.

## 4. The intellectual bridge the presentation can develop

The application makes one powerful argument: an information system controls much more than access to documents. It also determines which questions can be asked, whose account is seen, which differences count, and where a person can interrupt a conclusion. Kavian is a design response to that problem.

A promising presentation through-line is:

1. **The lived problem:** people experience decisions whose evidence and decision rules they cannot inspect or challenge.
2. **The design question:** how can a system increase public capacity to know and act without becoming a new, unaccountable authority?
3. **The proposed mechanism:** living dossiers turn incoming material into reviewable artifacts; a plural network of advisers and affected people can challenge them; AI helps organize the flow; the public sees evidence, alternatives, uncertainty, and contestable conclusions.
4. **The difficult choices:** who defines a valid question, selects evaluators, assigns weight, decides to publish, authorizes advocacy, protects vulnerable people, and can correct Kavian itself?
5. **The research horizon:** can a civic intelligence system change its framing when people show that its question, categories, or objective are wrong?

This is a candidate narrative for discussion, not a locked slide order. It creates continuity with *Redrawing the Space* while giving the audience something concrete to interrogate. The strongest demonstration may be a single **illustrative, explicitly fictional** public issue followed through a dossier and a challenge cycle; the example should be chosen later for clarity and relevance, rather than defaulting to water.

Theoretical links must remain disciplined. For example, Ashby's requisite variety may motivate plural sources and perspectives, but it does not prove that a diverse panel is correct. Beer's emphasis on what a system does can motivate outcome measurement, but it does not supply the measures. The human ability to refuse or reframe a question is a design requirement to specify and test, not an automatic property of having a review queue. Likewise, open-source components do not prove institutional independence; model confidence does not prove factual accuracy; and a many-sided presentation does not require treating unsupported claims as equal to well-supported ones.

The technical layer belongs in this story only where it explains a civic capability. Multi-agent orchestration could route a claim, preserve provenance, ask for the right kind of review, and incorporate corrections. Observability could show how an account changed. A small decision model, hosted like Jev or open-weight like Laya, could make a bounded routing or classification step faster, subject to review. None of these brand names is the human reason for Kavian to exist. The presentation should show what a person can understand, contest, and choose because of the architecture.

## 5. Presentation constraints and unresolved decisions

The presentation should be intellectually ambitious and mechanically clear. It needs to let someone understand what happens when an issue enters Kavian, what each human and machine component contributes, what comes back to the public, and how an error changes the record. It should acknowledge that governance is part of the technology: choosing evaluators, sources, categories, models, and release rules changes what people can see.

Questions to resolve during the presentation process:

- Who will attend, how long is the session, and is this an interview follow-up, project proposal, research seminar, or scholarship deliverable?
- What should the audience be able to assess afterward: intellectual originality, social need, implementability, research questions, or an invitation to collaborate?
- Which elements of Kaveh's personal story does he want to tell aloud, and which should remain private or only in the application?
- Which illustrative issue best shows the entire loop without implying that an unbuilt platform already operates?
- Which artifact types are essential for a first demonstration, and what minimum fields must each show (source, claim type, uncertainty, version, challenge route)?
- How are advisers selected, routed, compensated, protected, and, where appropriate, weighted? How can affected people challenge their framing?
- Which disagreements should be preserved as distinct positions, and which factual disputes can be resolved by further evidence?
- Where would Policy Delphi, structured elicitation, multi-criteria analysis, and sensitivity analysis genuinely help rather than add procedural theater?
- Which parts of the reported technical stack are running today, and what evidence can safely be shown? What must remain confidential?
- Can a decision model, Jev or an open-weight model such as Laya, handle the actual Persian-language and civic tasks, with acceptable error, latency, cost, data handling, and escalation? The slot has to stay replaceable either way.
- At what point can Kavian make a recommendation or support advocacy, and who is accountable for that step?
- What is the smallest honest prototype or research study that could test a pivotal assumption?

These are work items for the new presentation process. They are not reasons to delay developing a first narrative and visual structure when that work begins.

## 7. Working principle for everything built here

Present Kavian as a serious, revisable design inquiry with a compelling human reason to exist and enough mechanism to invite critique. Show exactly where people can supply evidence, challenge categories, reject an AI-assisted conclusion, and see a correction. State which powers Kavian would exercise and how they could be contested. Let the audience see both the ambition and the work still required to earn trust.
