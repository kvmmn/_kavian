## 4. Open research situation: creativity and artificial intelligence

The user explicitly rejected premature closure around a fixed project goal. This instruction is central.

The subject is not merely “Can ChatGPT be creative?” and not a prompt-engineering or generative-content project. It is an open research situation around creativity and artificial intelligence that may deepen technically, philosophically, conceptually, experimentally, socially, culturally, or semantically.

Do not assume in advance that the outcome must be a paper, experiment, metric, framework, prototype, product, or purely conceptual study. Those are possible forms, not predetermined goals.

### Generative questions

- What is creativity?
- How does novelty differ from creativity?
- How does statistical surprise differ from invention?
- Is recombination sufficient, or does creativity require conceptual transformation?
- Does creativity require intention?
- Does it require agency?
- Does it require memory, a world model, self-reflection, self-evaluation, or the capacity to revise goals?
- What is the relationship between creativity and representation, language, search, abstraction, embodiment, meaning, and self-modeling?
- Can a system meaningfully go beyond its training distribution, and what would “beyond” mean technically?
- Can an artificial system merely search an existing possibility space, or can it modify the space in which possibilities are defined?
- Is creativity a property of an individual agent, or can it emerge from a composite human–machine–environment–cultural system?
- In iterative human–AI work, where is the locus of creativity?
- How do memory, tool use, self-critique, iterative reasoning, evolutionary methods, or multi-agent interaction change creative behavior?
- Can creativity be operationalized computationally without reducing away its philosophical or cultural substance?
- When does generation become invention?

### Possible technical directions

- latent representations and movement through latent spaces;
- sampling versus search versus invention;
- computational novelty and surprise;
- world models and persistent memory;
- agentic architectures;
- reflection, critique, evaluation, and iterative revision;
- evolutionary search;
- tool-using systems;
- multi-agent systems and collective intelligence;
- mechanisms for changing objectives, representations, or problem spaces;
- comparative experiments across architectures;
- possible metrics or operational definitions, approached critically rather than treated as the necessary endpoint.

### Possible philosophical/conceptual directions

- creativity, invention, imagination, novelty, and transformation;
- intention, agency, authorship, subjectivity, and responsibility;
- intelligence as constructed, social, computational, and transformable;
- whether meaning is necessary for creativity;
- the difference between producing an unprecedented artifact and originating a new conceptual possibility;
- whether a “creator” must exist for creativity to occur;
- creativity as a relation or system-level property rather than an individual faculty;
- human–AI co-creation and distributed agency.

### Possible experimental forms

- compare how different memory or reflection mechanisms affect novelty and conceptual change;
- test whether agents can redefine constraints or invent new task representations;
- examine human–AI creative loops rather than evaluating isolated model outputs;
- compare single-agent and multi-agent creative processes;
- study whether evaluation models reinforce convention or enable transformation;
- investigate how tools and environments expand or constrain possibility spaces.

These are doors into the field, not a commitment to a particular endpoint.
